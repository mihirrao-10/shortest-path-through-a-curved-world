# Interview guide

## Thirty-second summary

> I built a C++20 implementation of the Heat Method for geodesic distance on triangle meshes. It validates an oriented halfedge mesh, assembles lumped mass and cotangent stiffness operators, factors the heat and pinned Poisson systems, reuses those factors across source queries, and traces a path across triangle faces. The primary example is a deterministic closed genus-one surface with a broad tunnel. A native exporter writes the actual geometry, heat frames, distance, Dijkstra baseline, gradients, landmarks, and routes for an interactive Three.js explanation.

## The central problem

Two points can be close in ambient three dimensional space while far apart along a surface. The straight Euclidean chord is not admissible if motion must remain on the surface.

Three distances appear in the project:

1. The ambient chord, which is not surface constrained.
2. Edge Dijkstra, which exactly solves the weighted mesh graph.
3. The Heat Method field, which approximates intrinsic surface distance and supports face-crossing paths.

The graph route can only use stored edge directions. The Heat route follows a piecewise linear scalar field across triangle interiors.

## Architecture

```text
procedural or OBJ geometry
        |
        v
validated halfedge TriangleMesh
        |
        v
mass, cotangent stiffness, face gradients
        |
        v
heat and pinned Poisson factorization
        |
        +--> Heat Method query --> distance and direction field
        |
        +--> edge Dijkstra --> graph distance and predecessors
        |
        v
native face tracer and versioned web export
        |
        v
Three.js and TypeScript presentation
```

The numerical engine is entirely C++20 and CPU based. Eigen provides sparse direct and iterative solvers. The browser does not assemble or solve the sparse systems.

## Halfedge representation

`TriangleMesh` stores:

- vertex position and one outgoing halfedge
- halfedge origin, twin, next, edge, and face
- one representative halfedge per edge
- one halfedge and three vertex indices per face
- cached vertex neighbors and incident faces

Construction rejects:

- empty geometry
- nonfinite coordinates
- out-of-range or repeated indices
- near-zero-area triangles unless explicit skipping is requested
- repeated directed edges
- inconsistent orientation across a shared edge
- nonmanifold edges with more than two incident faces

Validation then checks face cycles, twin symmetry, edge ownership, origin continuity, and cached incidence.

## Why genus one matters

The main surface is a real periodic toroidal mesh generated by C++, not a decorative browser shell.

At detail level 5:

- major segments: 160
- minor segments: 64
- vertices: 10,240
- faces: 20,480
- boundary edges: 0
- Euler characteristic: 0
- genus: 1

Low-frequency terms change the centerline radius, centerline height, tube scale, radial cross section, vertical cross section, and tangential position. The deformation remains smooth and small enough to preserve a broad tunnel and avoid self intersections.

Consistent parameter-grid winding is essential. A radial center-dot orientation heuristic would be wrong on the inner tunnel because correct outward normals there point toward the hole.

Landmarks are selected geometrically:

- source near a stable exterior anchor
- exterior target near an outer ridge anchor
- tunnel target near an inner-wall anchor
- far-side target chosen far from the source while remaining on the exterior band

This avoids depending on incidental raw index arithmetic.

## Discrete operators

### Lumped mass

For each face (f=(i,j,k)) with area (A_f), add (A_f/3) to the mass of each incident vertex. The result is a positive diagonal vector (M).

### Cotangent stiffness

For an edge opposite angle (alpha), the familiar local weight is proportional to (cot\alpha). Contributions from both incident triangles assemble a symmetric matrix (L) that approximates (-\Delta).

Expected properties include:

- (L=L^T)
- (L\mathbf 1\approx0)
- nonnegative quadratic energy despite possible negative individual cotangent contributions on obtuse triangles
- positive lumped mass for every nonisolated vertex

### Face gradients

For a triangle with barycentric basis functions (b_i), a piecewise linear scalar has constant face gradient

\[
\nabla u_f=\sum_i u_i\nabla b_i.
\]

The same basis gradients build the weak divergence load used by Poisson reconstruction.

## Heat Method derivation

Varadhan's relation motivates the method:

\[
d(x,y)^2=\lim_{t\to0}-4t\log k_t(x,y).
\]

The implementation uses the practical three-step construction rather than evaluating that limit directly.

### 1. Diffuse briefly

\[
(M+tL)u=M\delta,
\qquad t=h^2.
\]

`h` is the mean edge length. The mass-weighted impulse becomes a unit right-hand-side entry at the source.

### 2. Keep direction

\[
X_f=-\frac{\nabla u_f}{\lVert\nabla u_f\rVert}.
\]

Normalization removes decay magnitude and keeps the direction that points away from heat and toward increasing distance. Near-zero or nonfinite gradients are guarded.

### 3. Reconstruct distance

\[
L\phi=b_X,
\qquad
(b_X)_i=\sum_f A_f\nabla b_i\cdot X_f.
\]

The stiffness matrix has a constant nullspace. The implementation pins vertex zero, factors that matrix, solves, and shifts the field so the actual source value is zero.

## Sparse preprocessing and reuse

`HeatMethodSolver` records two preprocessing components:

1. operator assembly
2. heat-matrix and pinned-Poisson factorization

Changing a source changes only the right-hand sides. One solver instance can reuse both factors. The benchmark therefore measures one query and eight queries from distinct sources using the same solver.

The direct path uses Eigen `SimplicialLDLT`. The iterative path uses preconditioned conjugate gradient with incomplete Cholesky. Both calculate true relative residuals. Native tests require direct and iterative distance fields to agree within tolerance.

## Path extraction

The native tracer starts from a barycentric surface point and repeatedly:

1. evaluates the facewise distance gradient
2. follows the negative gradient
3. computes when a barycentric coordinate reaches zero
4. records the edge crossing
5. enters the adjacent face with a small numerical nudge
6. stops inside a source neighborhood

Guards detect face cycles, critical gradients, a boundary, and excessive steps. A monotone vertex fallback descends one-ring distance values when the continuous face trace becomes unreliable.

Tests require paths from both the exterior and inner tunnel presets to reach the source.

## Export and browser boundary

`world.bin` schema version 2 includes:

- positions and normals
- oriented triangle indices and face adjacency
- Heat Method and Dijkstra distances
- Dijkstra predecessors
- six logarithmically encoded heat frames
- sampled face directions
- source and three target landmarks
- native Heat and edge paths for every preset
- native chord, Heat-path, and edge-path lengths

`world.meta.json` records topology, residuals, timings, encoding, and engine identity. TypeScript validates counts, version, topology, and landmark agreement before constructing the scene.

For a preset, the browser displays native routes and lengths directly. For pointer placement, it traces the exported scalar and predecessor fields to keep interaction local. That presentation step does not replace the C++ field solve.

## Common questions

### Why use the Heat Method if Dijkstra is faster here?

They solve different discretized problems. Dijkstra is exact on the edge graph. The Heat Method reconstructs a surface field and allows route extraction across face interiors. It also amortizes preprocessing when many sources share a mesh.

### Is the Heat route exact?

No. It inherits field discretization and path-tracing error. It should be described as an approximate intrinsic route.

### Why can the measured Heat polyline exceed the graph route?

The theoretical continuous geodesic is no longer than a route restricted to edges, but the exported amber polyline is an approximate descent trace. Field and tracer errors can make that sampled path longer than the exact graph route on a finite mesh.

### Why pin vertex zero rather than the source?

Any single gauge constraint removes the constant nullspace. Pinning a fixed vertex makes preprocessing independent of the source. Shifting the final field then puts zero at the requested source.

### What happens at a cut locus?

The exact distance is nonsmooth there. A discrete gradient may become ambiguous or weak. The tracer detects cycles and critical faces and can switch to monotone vertex descent.

### What is the biggest remaining numerical limitation?

Mesh and operator quality. Obtuse triangles can create negative cotangent weights, one global diffusion time may not suit every local scale, and the route inherits approximation error from both reconstruction and tracing.
