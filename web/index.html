The Shortest Path Through a Curved World

How heat reconstructs distance on triangulated multi-handle surfaces.

An explorer stands on a folded two-handle world. An amber beacon waits across its outer ridge, shared neck, and basin. What is the shortest route that never leaves the surface?

Genus counts handles: Genus 1 has one, Genus 2 has two, and Genus 3 has three. For a connected closed orientable triangulated surface, . Switching genus changes global topology, not merely cosmetic shape.

Follow the question ↓

Why not draw a straight line?

The curved world is a two-dimensional surface embedded in ordinary three-dimensional space. That surrounding room is the ambient space. A straight line is allowed to move anywhere in that room, even where the surface is absent.

This segment is the ambient chord from start to beacon . It is shortest in Euclidean 3D, but on a multi-handle world it can leave the surface, cut through a tube, or cross empty space inside the hole.

A surface path is a continuous curve for which every point lies on the surface. Such a path is admissible, meaning it is a route the explorer is allowed to walk. The chord usually is not.

How can a computer hold the surface?

A triangle mesh stores vertices, the sampled points; edges, the links between them; and triangular faces, the local surface pieces. Every face is flat, while halfedge connectivity joins them into one closed curved surface without a boundary.

More, better-shaped triangles usually reduce discretization error, but increase assembly, factorization, rendering, and storage costs. Thin or badly angled triangles also make cotangent operators less reliable.

The central neck is saddle-like: along two tangent directions, the surface bends in opposite ways. That geometric description is visible in the generated shape; this project does not claim a separate numerical Gaussian-curvature computation.

Edge Dijkstra sees only the graph of vertices and edges. It finds the exact shortest route in that graph, but it cannot cut across a triangle interior. Its result can zigzag or change with the available edge directions, so it is a baseline rather than the continuous surface answer.

The edge route is not an exact continuous geodesic on the polyhedral surface.

Warm the beacon for an instant.

Heat begins at the shared source, then diffuses through the connected surface. It travels around each handle, through the neck, through the basin, and across the outer relief because the discrete equation has no route through ambient space.

Can the flow of heat reveal distance?

Six exported diffusion states, solved by the C++ engine.

Warmth becomes direction.

On each face, piecewise-linear heat values form a constant local slope. The heat gradient points toward the hot source; negating it points outward in the direction of increasing distance. Normalizing keeps that direction while discarding the rapid decay in magnitude. The route ultimately follows the reconstructed distance field in the opposite direction.

Those facewise directions need not fit one scalar field perfectly. A Poisson solve finds the distance-like field whose gradient agrees with them as closely as the discretization allows. Tracing that field downhill across face interiors then yields an approximate route to the beacon.

The Heat Method, one stage at a time

The method does not search through complete paths. It first asks how a very brief pulse spreads from the source, then turns that local response into a global distance-like field.

1

Create a brief heat impulse. The vector identifies the source. The lumped mass matrix distributes vertex values according to surrounding area. The stiffness matrix discretizes the positive-semidefinite Laplace-Beltrami operator, and is a short diffusion time. The solution is temperature.

Choosing , where is mean edge length, ties the pulse to mesh scale. At short times, points reached sooner tend to be closer along the surface. Too much diffusion blurs local distance information; too little magnifies discretization noise.

2

Read direction. Each face has a constant piecewise-linear heat gradient. Since heat is largest near the source, points toward hotter values. The minus sign turns it outward, so aligns with increasing distance. Normalization removes decay in magnitude while retaining direction. The final route later follows decreasing distance back toward the source.

3

Reconstruct distance. Independent facewise arrows may not be the gradient of one globally consistent scalar function. The load is their weak divergence. The Poisson solve finds a scalar field whose gradient best agrees with over the mesh.

Adding a constant to every value would leave all gradients unchanged, so the system otherwise has one arbitrary degree of freedom. The implementation pins one reference value, then shifts the solved field so the source distance is zero. This plain reference choice is often called gauge fixing.

4

Trace across faces. The native tracer starts from an authored face and barycentric point, follows decreasing to a barycentric edge crossing, enters the adjacent face, and repeats until it reaches the source neighborhood. Critical regions can stop a face tracer, so every published preset is validated to reach the source without fallback.

The weight on an interior edge comes from the two angles and opposite that shared edge. These local angles assemble the discrete operator. Poor triangle quality can therefore degrade the approximation even when the topology is correct.

What remains approximate

  • The Heat Method result is an approximation.
  • The triangle mesh discretizes the intended smooth surface.
  • A face tracer can encounter critical regions.
  • Cotangent operators depend on triangle quality.
  • Dijkstra is restricted to mesh edges.
  • A multi-handle world can have nearly equal routes in different homotopy classes.
  • All numerical computation in this project is CPU-only.

Under the hood

The page is static, but its geometry and numerical fields are not browser inventions. A deterministic native pipeline produces the surface, solves the field, validates the routes, and writes the files rendered here.

01

C++20 multi-genus generation

A project-specific implicit field thickens a deterministic chain of rounded loops. Native marching tetrahedra reuses edge intersections, smooths tangentially, reprojects to the level set, and verifies orientation, manifold structure, Euler characteristic, triangle quality, and deterministic landmarks.

02

Eigen sparse solves

Lumped areas form the mass matrix, cotangent weights form the symmetric stiffness matrix, and barycentric gradients supply the facewise differential operators. Eigen runs reusable direct and iterative CPU paths and reports relative residuals.

03

Deterministic native export

The exporter writes compact indexed geometry, adjacency, six heat states, the distance field, Dijkstra distances and predecessors, gradient samples, semantic route presets, native trace measurements, topology, and residual metadata.

04

TypeScript interpretation

TypeScript validates binary schema version 3, topology, path ranges, and measurements. The displayed green path is the native C++ trace; an independent browser tracer remains a correctness check rather than the authoritative geometry.

05

Persistent presentation

Three.js renders the selected native world on one reusable canvas while the damped camera orbits it. KaTeX emits HTML and MathML notation. Both libraries are bundled into the static GitHub Pages build, including the local STIX Two Text files used by the prose.

06

Numerical and browser tests

Native tests validate topology, operators, residuals, route reconstruction, and byte-for-byte deterministic export. Playwright exercises every route, heat replay, comparison, camera input, accessibility state, responsive stacking, fallback behavior, and dark surfaces.

Limitations

The surface is discretized, the Heat Method is approximate, and cotangent accuracy depends on triangle quality. Dijkstra is edge-restricted. Face tracing can fail near critical regions, although every published preset is verified without fallback. Distinct routes around a handle can be nearly equal. The project uses CPU computation throughout.
