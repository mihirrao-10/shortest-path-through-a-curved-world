The Shortest Path Through a Curved World

How heat reconstructs distance on a triangulated surface.

Imagine you wake on an alien world folded into two handles. A transport beacon glows across the surface. The straight line to it cuts through empty space, but your suit cannot leave the ground. What is the shortest route you can actually walk?

The journey opens on a folded world with two handles. You can later change its topology, but the rule never changes: every valid step must remain on the surface. Genus counts handles, and the selector spans one through three. For a connected, closed, orientable triangulated surface, . Switching genus changes global topology, not merely cosmetic shape.

Why not draw a straight line?

The first temptation is a straight line. The curved world has two dimensions and sits inside ordinary space with three dimensions. That surrounding room is the ambient space. A straight line may move anywhere in that room, including places where the surface is absent.

This segment is the ambient chord from start to beacon . It is shortest in Euclidean 3D, but it can leave the surface, cut through a tube, or cross empty space inside a hole.

A surface path is a continuous curve whose every point lies on the surface. Such a path is admissible, meaning it is a route the explorer is allowed to walk. The chord usually is not.

How can a computer hold the surface?

To remain on the world, the computer first needs a map. A triangle mesh stores sampled points called vertices, links called edges, and triangular faces. Every face is flat. Halfedge connectivity, a directed edge record that links neighboring faces, joins them into one closed surface without a boundary.

More triangles with better shape quality usually reduce discretization error, but increase assembly, factorization, rendering, and storage costs. Thin or badly angled triangles also make cotangent operators less reliable.

The central neck is shaped like a saddle: along two tangent directions, the surface bends in opposite ways. That shape is visible in the generated geometry. This project does not claim a separate numerical computation of Gaussian curvature.

The displayed Dijkstra baseline first connects the interior start to its nearest face vertex. From that vertex onward, Dijkstra finds the exact shortest route in the mesh edge graph, whose restricted directions can make the result zigzag. It is a baseline rather than the continuous surface answer.

The edge route is not an exact continuous geodesic on the polyhedral surface.

Release heat

Now warm the beacon for an instant. Heat begins there, then diffuses through the connected surface. It travels around handles, through necks, and across relief because the discrete equation offers no route through ambient space.

The animation uses additional C++ solves across a range of diffusion times, so the final frame visibly reaches every authored route start. That range extends well beyond the short field used by the Heat Method path solve, but the display times never replace it.

Warmth becomes direction

Warmth first reveals direction, then a distance field reveals a route. On each face, heat values that vary linearly form a constant local slope. A gradient is the direction and rate of steepest increase. The heat gradient points toward the hot source, so negating it points outward toward increasing distance. Normalizing keeps direction while discarding magnitude.

Those facewise directions need not fit one scalar field perfectly. A Poisson solve finds the field whose gradient agrees with them as closely as the discretization allows. Tracing that distance approximation downhill across face interiors yields an approximate route to the beacon.

The Heat Method, one stage at a time

The method does not search through complete paths. It asks how a brief pulse spreads from the source, then turns that local response into a global approximation of distance.

1

Create a brief heat impulse. The vector marks the source. The mass matrix assigns area to vertices. The stiffness matrix measures changes across neighboring vertices. It discretizes the positive semidefinite surface Laplacian, the surface version of the operator that describes diffusion. The solution is temperature.

Choosing , where is mean edge length, ties the pulse to mesh scale. Too much diffusion blurs local distance information. Too little magnifies discretization noise.

2

Read direction. Since heat is largest near the source, points toward hotter values. The minus sign turns it outward, so aligns with increasing distance. The final route follows decreasing distance back toward the source.

3

Reconstruct distance. Divergence measures the net flow out of a local region. The load records how the arrow field pairs with every basis gradient. The Poisson equation links those pairings to a scalar field whose gradient best agrees with over the mesh.

Adding a constant leaves every gradient unchanged, so the system has one arbitrary degree of freedom. The implementation pins one reference value, then shifts the solved field so the source distance is zero. This reference choice is called gauge fixing.

4

Trace across faces. The C++ tracer starts from an exported face and barycentric point, follows decreasing to an edge, enters the adjacent face, and repeats until it reaches the source neighborhood. Critical regions can stop a face tracer, so every published preset is validated to reach the source without fallback.

The weight on an interior edge comes from the two angles opposite that edge. These local angles assemble the discrete operator, so poor triangle quality can degrade the approximation even when topology is correct.

What remains approximate

  • The Heat Method result is an approximation.
  • The triangle mesh discretizes a smooth surface.
  • A face tracer can encounter critical regions.
  • Cotangent operators depend on triangle quality.
  • Dijkstra is restricted to mesh edges.
  • Routes can loop around handles in topologically different ways, called different homotopy classes.
  • All geometry generation and numerical solves in this project run on the CPU.

Heat reveals a way home.

Under the hood

The page is static, but its geometry and numerical fields are not browser inventions. A deterministic C++ pipeline produces the surface, solves the field, validates the routes, and writes the files rendered here.

C++20 generation across three genera

An implicit field designed for this project thickens one, two, or three adjacent rounded loops, preserving the irregular ring and worlds with two folded loops. Marching tetrahedra reuses edge intersections, smooths tangentially, reprojects to the level set, and verifies orientation, manifold structure, Euler characteristic, triangle quality, and landmarks.

Eigen sparse solves

Lumped areas form the mass matrix, cotangent weights form the symmetric stiffness matrix, and barycentric gradients supply facewise differential operators. Eigen runs reusable direct and iterative CPU paths.

Deterministic C++ export

The exporter writes indexed geometry, adjacency, nine native heat display states, distance, Dijkstra data, gradient samples, route presets, C++ trace measurements, topology, and solver metadata.

TypeScript interpretation

TypeScript validates binary schema version 3, topology, path ranges, and measurements. The displayed green paths are C++ traces. A browser tracer remains an independent check, not the source of the displayed geometry.

Persistent presentation

Three.js renders one validated world on a reusable canvas while the damped camera orbits it. KaTeX emits HTML and MathML. Both libraries and STIX Two Text are bundled into the static GitHub Pages build.

Numerical and browser tests

Native tests validate topology, operators, routes, and deterministic export. Relative residuals are normalized checks of how closely each sparse system satisfies its equation. Browser tests cover journey state, camera input, accessibility, responsive layouts, and fallback behavior.

Boundaries and limitations

The mesh discretizes a smooth surface, the Heat Method is approximate, and cotangent accuracy depends on triangle quality. Dijkstra remains restricted to edges. Face tracing can fail near critical regions, although every published preset reaches the source without fallback. Distinct homotopy classes can have nearly equal lengths. Geometry generation and numerical solves run on the CPU.
