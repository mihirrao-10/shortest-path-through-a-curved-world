The Shortest Path Through a Curved World

A C++ Heat Method Geodesic Solver on Triangle Meshes

A C++20 implementation constructs discrete surface operators, reuses sparse factorizations, traces paths across triangle faces, and exports real numerical fields to this Three.js explanation.

Opening 01 / Constraint

The ambient straight line fails.

The violet chord is shortest in the surrounding three dimensional space, but it cuts through the world. A valid route must stay on the two dimensional surface, including the inner wall of its tunnel.

  • The ambient chord is not surface constrained.
  • Edge Dijkstra solves the weighted mesh graph exactly.
  • The Heat Method approximates intrinsic surface distance.

Geometry / 01

Curved surface and intrinsic distance

Intrinsic distance only measures motion allowed within the surface. Bending the mesh in ambient space does not change that metric unless edge lengths or connectivity change.

Edge Dijkstra route

Dijkstra is exact for the weighted graph formed by mesh edges. It cannot cross a face interior, so its result depends on triangulation directions and resolution.

Tunnel and topology

This is a closed, oriented genus-one mesh. Its Euler characteristic is zero, there is no boundary, and the inner tunnel is part of the same numerical surface as the exterior ridge.

Face gradients

Heat Method / 05

Poisson reconstruction

finds a scalar field whose gradient best matches the normalized directions. Vertex zero is pinned to fix the constant nullspace, then the source value is shifted to zero.

Heat Method / 06

Face by face path tracing

The tracer follows distance downhill, solves for the next barycentric edge crossing, enters the adjacent face, and stops in a source neighborhood. Cycle and critical point guards use a monotone fallback.

Heat Method / 07

Limitations and approximation error

Accuracy depends on triangle quality, mesh resolution, diffusion time, operator discretization, and path extraction. This is not an exact polyhedral geodesic algorithm, and cut loci can create difficult descent fields.

C++ Engine / 02

Cotangent Laplacian and lumped mass

Triangle cotangents assemble a symmetric stiffness matrix. One third of each incident triangle area forms the diagonal lumped mass. Both are checked for finite values and expected invariants.

Implementation summary

Under the hood

The numerical engine is intentionally focused: modern C++20, discrete differential geometry, sparse CPU linear algebra, deterministic exports, and a browser presentation of those results.

01

Genus-one generator

A periodic two parameter grid forms a deformed torus. Low frequency radial, vertical, and tangential terms keep the broad tunnel visible without self intersections.

02

Validated topology

Consistent winding produces finite exterior and tunnel normals. Tests require no boundary, positive areas, manifold halfedges, Euler characteristic zero, and valid landmarks.

03

Sparse systems

The mass and cotangent stiffness matrices define diffusion and pinned Poisson reconstruction. Assembly and factorization are measured separately.

04

Reusable solves

Matrix factorizations are cached by the solver and reused for changing source impulses. Direct and iterative CPU results are compared by residual and field agreement.

05

Path extraction

Native tests trace both exterior and inner tunnel targets. Exported preset polylines and graph routes supply the browser's measured comparisons.

06

Deterministic presentation

The Three.js scene renders only the C++ generated mesh and fields. Pointer placement traces the exported scalar field for interactive inspection.

Genuine limitations

The Heat Method is approximate. Quality depends on mesh resolution, triangle shape, diffusion time, cotangent behavior, and path extraction. Factor reuse reduces repeated setup work. The benchmark reports only its measured timings.
