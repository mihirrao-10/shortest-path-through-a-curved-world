A question in geometry

The Shortest Path Through a Curved World

How heat reveals a route across a folded surface.

An explorer stands on an uneven world. A beacon waits across its ridges, basin, and narrow pass. Which surface path gets home first?

I · The tempting answer

The ambient chord cheats.

A straight segment is shortest in the surrounding 3D space, but it cuts through the world. The explorer must stay on the surface, so the admissible geometry is different.

II · Triangle mesh and Dijkstra

A curved world, represented locally.

Oriented triangles approximate the unequal lobes, strong ridge, deep basin, saddle, and compressed channel. Each face is flat; their connectivity carries the global shape.

Dijkstra supplies a useful baseline by walking only along mesh edges. Its route is intrinsic to the edge graph, but its turns inherit graph directions and can be biased by triangulation.

The edge route is not an exact continuous geodesic on the polyhedral surface.

IV · Release heat

Warm the beacon for an instant.

Heat begins at the shared source, then diffuses through the surface. It bends around the ridge, spreads into the basin, and passes through the neck because it cannot leave the mesh.

V · Read the direction field

Warmth becomes direction.

On each face, heat values form a local slope. Reverse and normalize its gradient to keep direction while discarding magnitude. A Poisson solve then integrates those local directions into one global distance field.

Tracing that field downhill across faces yields an approximate surface path back to the beacon.

The weight on an interior edge comes from the two angles opposite that edge. These local triangle angles assemble the discrete surface operator, so poor triangle quality can degrade the approximation.

The Heat Method approximates geodesic distance; it does not return an exact polyhedral geodesic. Mesh quality matters, edge-graph Dijkstra has directional bias, and the browser tracer uses a guarded fallback near critical points. CUDA is compile-covered, but no CUDA runtime measurement is claimed.

IX · Answer

Heat reveals a way home.

Diffuse from the beacon, normalize the facewise gradient, reconstruct distance, then follow that field downhill across the actual surface. The result is approximate, measurable, and repeatable for every authored start.

For the technically curious

Under the hood

A C++20 geometry engine produces the mesh, operators, distance field, route presets, and exported measurements. This section separates exact implementation facts from numerical approximation.

01

Procedural world

One deterministic, closed genus-zero mesh combines anisotropic lobes with a ridge, basin, saddle, and narrow channel while remaining suitable for cotangent operators.

02

Mesh representation

An oriented halfedge structure stores faces, edges, twins, incidence, cached one-rings, and boundary state. Construction rejects invalid orientation and degenerate triangles.

03

Discrete operators

Lumped vertex areas form the mass matrix. Triangle cotangents assemble a symmetric stiffness matrix; barycentric gradients supply scalar gradients and weak divergence loads.

04

Numerical design

Eigen supplies reference direct and iterative solve paths with reported relative residuals. Heat and pinned Poisson factorizations are reusable between source queries.

05

Path extraction

The tracer follows a piecewise-linear face gradient, transfers across barycentric edge crossings, and stops near the source. Guards can switch to a monotone vertex fallback.

06

Optional CUDA path

The optional backend keeps CSR matrices and work vectors resident, using cuSPARSE, cuBLAS, preconditioned conjugate gradients, and custom geometry and update kernels in double precision.

Limitations

Approximation error depends on mesh resolution and triangle quality. Dijkstra measures edge-graph distance. The face tracer may use a guarded fallback near critical points. CUDA has compile coverage in its established workflow, but no runtime result is presented without compatible hardware.
